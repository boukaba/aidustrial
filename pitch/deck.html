Aidustrial

Multi-AI-Agent operations for industrial agro-complexes

Seven specialist AI Agents run the operation around the clock against a live digital twin, reason in the open, and stop at a human gate before anything consequential. Built entirely around the Baladna project in Adrar, and adaptable to any industry that runs on specialists.

03
The problem
The problem

An operation this size produces more telemetry
than any team can turn into timely decisions.

Volume and variety

Six domains, all speaking at once

Feed, water, herd, milk, machinery, supply. Pivots, silos, the parlour, weather stations and laboratories, each is somebody's whole job.

Coupling

A decision in one lands in another

A bearing fails, a pivot stops, a field dries, the herd loses feed. Every late decision compounds in water, in yield, and in animal welfare.

Timing

The clock starts before the herd lands

30,000 Holsteins arrive by air from November 2026. The observing has to be running before the first animal is on the ground.

This is not a data problem. It is a decision problem, and it is the same shape in any large industrial operation.

The orchestrator
No AI Agent calls another directly. The orchestrator wakes the specialists whose domains changed, routes their questions through a shared blackboard, and will not let anything reach the gate until the swarm has converged.
Ask any AI Agent
Click a specialist and put a question to it. It answers from what it is reading now, the documents it works from, and its own history, then shows the sources it used.
05
How it thinks
How it thinks

Two models. One decides, one explains.
Neither sets the risk.

The Jev model, decides
Typed judgement, with the odds attached
Structured questions against a closed vocabulary of actions, answered as a validated verdict in under one second. Every call carries the probability it was working from.
The LLM model, explains
The reasoning you can argue with
Plain-language explanation a manager can read aloud and challenge, sourced from the same readings the decision was made on. No black box to take on trust.
Telemetry from everywhere
Pivot controllers, silo load cells, well yields, herd vitals, the parlour, weather stations and fleet positions feed one digital twin, plus live results from the food, water, milk and veterinary labs.
Retrieval, with citations
Each AI Agent answers from eight domain documents it can cite, thresholds, protocols and reference ranges, so an explanation points at PIVOT-MAINTENANCE §Vibration, not at the model's opinion.
06
The gate
The gate

Autonomy where it is safe.
A human specialist where it is not.

If you approve
Cooling starts in the barns most under heat stress. Those cows eat and milk normally again within hours.
If you do nothing
Rumination suppression precedes a milk-yield loss that does not recover that lactation.
01
Runs alone
Lighting and work orders at any risk: they change nothing physical. A technician is dispatched 24/7 without anyone answering a prompt.
02
Auto below a tight ceiling
Ventilation and misting below 25, reversible within minutes. Every other action defaults to 40.
03
Always a human
Water transfers, pivot restarts, shedding load, culling. No score makes them safe. The risk number is ours, never the model's.
07
What happens next
What happens next

Next three months: from a twin
onto one real barn group.

01
Wire the tool boundary to live telemetry
Pivot controllers, silo load cells and the laboratory systems already producing food, water and milk results daily.
02
Run four weeks in shadow mode
All seven AI Agents live beside the existing team, every decision logged next to the human's.
03
Harden it for industrial deployment
PostgreSQL replacing SQLite, row-level multi-tenant isolation, structured logging with audit retention, and Neo4j for the cross-domain knowledge graph.
04
Then reinforcement learning
Once the logs are real, they become a dispatch policy that improves on what the AI Agents choose today.

The core is already built, this is the last mile. Success is one number: decisions made earlier than they are today.

Then we would like an introduction to Baladna. The same architecture fits any specialist industry.
