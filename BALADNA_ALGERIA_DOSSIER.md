# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Baladna Algeria is a **USD 3.5 billion** integrated dairy and milk powder production project located on a **117,000 hectare** concession in **Adrar Province**, 1,543 km south of Algiers in the Algerian Sahara (~90 km from the provincial capital).

It is the flagship of Baladna Q.P.S.C.'s **"From Qatar to the World"** international expansion strategy, and is routinely described as **the world's largest integrated dairy farming project**.

**Headline targets:**
- **270,000 dairy cows** (primarily Holstein) at full build-out
- **1.7 billion litres** of milk annually
- **~100,000-200,000 tonnes** of milk powder annually (figures vary by source; target of ~50% of Algeria's national powdered milk demand)
- **2.5 million tonnes** of annual forage production
- **5,000 direct jobs** (Baladna has more recently cited "15,000+")

**Ownership:** 51% Baladna (Qatar) / 49% Fonds National d'Investissement (Algerian state sovereign investment fund)
**Contracted to date:** **>USD 1.135 billion** across two tranches (Jul 2025: >$500M; Apr 2026: >$635M)
**Status (Oct 2026):** Under construction. Wells drilled, first crops harvested, civil works underway. Cattle airlift begins November 2026. **First milk powder targeted late 2027.**

**Critical caveat:** the project's "USD 3.5bn foreign investment" framing is contested. On Baladna's own audited accounts, the project company held only ~**USD 223 million** of assets at 31 Dec 2025 with **zero revenue**, and Baladna publicly states that **Algerian banks + the FNI provide the majority of the financing** while Baladna controls management and the board. See [§8](#8-financing-structure) and [§13](#13-critical-analysis-risks--controversies).

## 3.2 The 219,000 vs 117,000 hectare discrepancy

**The official project website, [baladna-algeria.dz](https://www.baladna-algeria.dz/), states the project spans 219,000 hectares, twice the 117,000 ha figure used in every other document.**

| Source | Area |
|---|---|
| **baladna-algeria.dz** (official project site) | **219,000 ha** |
| Baladna Q.P.S.C. Annual Report 2025 | 117,000 ha |
| Baladna April 2024 framework agreement press release | 117,000 ha |
| GEA Group press release (29 Jul 2025) | 117,000 ha |
| Gulf Times (Aug 2025) | 117,000 ha |
| TSA-Algérie (28 Jul 2025) | 117,000 ha |
| FPT Industrial press release (23 Jun 2026) | 117,000 ha |
| Twala / Algeria Invest (Apr 2026) | 117,000 ha |
| GRAIN land-deal database | 117,000 ha |

This is not a rounding difference. **219,000 ha is the number Algerian job applicants are being recruited against**, while shareholders, lenders and the Algerian state are working with 117,000 ha.

**Unverified explanations** (none confirmed by any source): a broader catchment area including outlying forage plots outside the concession perimeter; a typo (219 vs 117); a later land extension not yet reflected in shareholder documents; or a figure that includes leased third-party land.

**Why it matters:** if 219,000 ha is the real operating footprint, the per-hectare capital intensity drops (~$15,982/ha vs $29,910/ha), which materially changes the Tafelwit "USD 700,000 per job" critique. If 117,000 ha is correct, the recruitment site is materially overstating the footprint to job candidates.

## 3.3 Employment conflict: 1,000 vs 5,000 vs 15,000

| Figure | Source | Date |
|---|---|---|
| **5,000 direct jobs** | Algerian government / APS / Baladna PR | Apr 2024, Sep 2024 |
| **~1,200 US jobs** (cattle airlift supply chain) | Baladna PR | Apr 2026 |
| **15,000+ jobs** | Baladna Q.P.S.C. press release | Apr 2026 |
| **1,000 posts by 2027** | **Official project site, recruitment campaign** | **Sep 2026** |

The company opened a **national recruitment tour on 2 September 2026** (starting 5 September in Adrar) targeting **1,000 positions** with staggered start dates tracking the site's ramp-up. Work locations: **Adrar and Algiers**.

The 1,000 figure is a *recruitment tranche*, not a lifetime total, but it is **5×-15× lower** than the headline employment promises. Given the recruitment is live now and the earlier figures date from 2024, the operative number for anyone assessing labour-market impact appears to be nearer 1,000 than 15,000.

**This materially undercuts Tafelwit's headline critique** of "USD 700,000 per job" (which used 5,000). At 1,000 direct jobs the figure is USD 3.5M per job, though that is arguably the *worse* number, since it means the capital intensity per actual job created is far higher than claimed.

## 3.4 The operating model as described by the company

The official site frames the project as four integrated links **on a single site**:

> *"La force du projet, c'est qu'il **n'importe rien de ce qui peut être produit localement** : quatre maillons reliés, sur un même site, à Adrar, du fourrage à la poudre."*
> ("The strength of the project is that it **imports nothing that can be produced locally**: four links connected, on the same site, in Adrar, from fodder to powder.")

| Link | Description |
|---|---|
| **01, Fodder** | Grown on site, **starting with barley**. Bores completed, irrigation in service, **first barley harvest at Tamest, May 2026** |
| **02, Herd** | Holsteins in **climate-controlled barns**, under permanent veterinary supervision. First 30,000 cows arrive November 2026 |
| **03, Milk** | **Automated milking measuring quantity and quality at every milking, for every cow** |
| **04, Powder** | Processed on site and **distributed to dairies across the whole territory**. First production expected end-2027. *"À la place de l'importation : une poudre algérienne pour des laiteries algériennes"* |

**Qatar benchmark cited by the company:** model launched 2014; **2.4 million m²**; **24,000+ Holsteins**; **>85% of Qatar's fresh milk**.
⚠️ Note: this ">85% of fresh milk" figure is **lower** than the 96.1% market share cited in trade press (FoodNavigator), likely a needs-vs-market-share distinction, not a contradiction.

**Go-to-market:** powder is sold **B2B to Algerian dairies/processors**, not direct to retail. This matters because it means the ONIL offtake is the anchor customer and there is no Baladna-owned retail distribution brand in Algeria.

**Recruitment categories** (reveals the intended operating model and the skill base required):

| Pillar | Roles sought |
|---|---|
| **Production végétale** | Agronomes · Conducteurs d'engins et de **pivots** · Techniciens électromécaniciens · Hydrologues et automaticiens |
| **Élevage et production laitière** | Vétérinaires · Spécialistes de l'alimentation animale · Techniciens de santé du troupeau · Encadrement d'exploitation et de cheptel |
| **Transformation industrielle** | Ingénieurs procédé et automatisme · Opérateurs de l'usine de lait en poudre · Techniciens contrôle qualité · Techniciens maintenance |

## 3.6 Algeria's milk import context (the rationale)

- Algeria is the **world's 2nd-3rd largest importer of powdered milk** (Baladna says 3rd)
- ~**USD 800m/year** of dairy imports (USDA)
- In 2025 Algeria became **Uruguay's largest dairy customer, USD 343 million**
- Algeria imports >400,000 t/yr from EU, New Zealand, Uruguay, Argentina
- Algeria already spends **>DA 18 billion/year** supporting the domestic milk sector
- Other national initiatives: **"Lait en Algérie, un pari de 3.5 milliards de dollars"**

# 8. FINANCING STRUCTURE

## 8.1 ⚠️ The equity/debt split is publicly CONTRADICTED

This is the single most important unresolved issue.

### Version A, Algerian state press (equity-led)
> *"Le financement du projet est conjoint, à hauteur de **51% de la part de la joint-venture** et **49 % de crédits de banques algériennes**."*
>, APS via Radio Algérie, 20 Sept 2024

### Version B, Baladna CFO Saifullah Khan, Q2 2024 call (debt-led)

## 12.4 🚩 Critical analytical flag, margins are NOT operating margins

Baladna's reported EBITDA and net profit include **fair-value gains on financial assets at FVTPL**:

- FY2025 P&L: **Gain on investment in financial assets at FVTPL: QAR 408,007,466** (vs QAR 37,956,908 in FY2024). Operating profit QAR 618.5M; net profit QAR 538.8M → **the fair-value gain alone is ~76% of net profit**
- H1 2026: QAR 254,453,948 fair-value gain vs net profit QAR 361.9M
- Q2 2026 alone: **EBITDA margin 108.9%**, **net profit margin 91.3%**, mathematically impossible for a pure operating business

The company labels this the **"Strategically Diversified Investment Portfolio."**

**Consequence:** the headline "QAR 770M EBITDA / 60.7% margin" and the "QAR 1.4bn EBITDA by 2030" target are **not comparable** to a dairy manufacturer's operating EBITDA. Any peer benchmarking against Lactalis, Almarai or Savencia is invalid without stripping this out.

# 13. CRITICAL ANALYSIS, RISKS & CONTROVERSIES

## 13.1 Twala (Algeria), "the state sold a framework of exception" (24 Apr 2026)

> *"Présenté comme un investissement étranger de 3,5 milliards de dollars, le projet Baladna à Adrar est surtout un chantier sous haute protection publique : **déchouché garanti par l'ONIL sur 20 ans, paiement sécurisé sur 20 ans, exonération fiscale de 10 ans, dette subventionnée, foncier massif et appareil administratif mobilisé.** À ce niveau de garanties, la question est aussi : pourquoi pas un opérateur algérien ?"*

> *"Si l'État algérien garantit l'achat via l'ONIL pendant 20 ans, sécurise le paiement pendant 20 ans, allège l'impôt pendant 10 ans, facilite l'accès à une dette subventionnée et mobilise ses entreprises publiques pour l'infrastructure, alors **une part importante du risque financier et industriel est déplacée vers la sphère algérienne**. Le projet reste privé dans sa vitrine, mais sa bancabilité dépend largement de la puissance publique."*

> *"Un projet privé en vitrine, un risque largement public."*

## 13.2 GRAIN, the geopolitical framing (Jan 2025)

- **Water:** *"cette ferme aura besoin de plus de 1 700 milliards de litres d'eau provenant d'un **aquifère non renouvelable**"*
- **Control:** *"Algerian banks and the Algerian National Investment Fund will provide **three-quarters of the funds**... the management of the farm and its board of directors are controlled by Baladna, through its 51% equity stake"*
- **Loyalty:** *"Baladna is a 'strategic company' of the Qatari government and its stated first priority is Qatar's food security, not Algeria's food needs. While both sides speak about Algeria's food security to the media, the company speaks about a 'significant return on investments' to its investors"*
- **Scale:** the Algeria project is *"ten times larger than the massive Baladna dairy farm in Qatar that it is modelled on"*
- **Small farmers:** *"these developments have been above all detrimental to small-farmers, who face reduced access to irrigation water and often have no choice but to engage in contract farming with corporations"*

*Baladna's rebuttal:* monitoring of soil moisture/salinity via sensors, weather stations and satellite imagery; "best irrigation methods to minimize excessive water use"; 186 drilling permits and 45 completed wells by Q4 2025.

## 13.3 Tafelwit, "triple aberration" (June 2026)

- **Economic non-viability:** estimated production cost **3-6× the price of imported European/Oceanian milk**
- **Capital-per-job:** USD 3.5bn / 5,000 jobs = **USD 700,000 per job** (vs. <USD 10,000 textiles, USD 5,000-15,000 market gardening)
- **Hydrology:** deep pumping from the **Albian fossil aquifer**; 500-700M m³/yr Phase 1 → **2.5-4 billion m³/yr at full capacity** (vs. ~1.2bn m³/yr total flow of the Oued Cheliff, Algeria's longest river)
- **No public EIA:** *"aucune étude d'impact environnemental et social (EIES) indépendante et rendue publique n'a été publiée avant le lancement des travaux"*
- **Opacity:** benefit-sharing terms, investor guarantees, water drilling rights and subsidy commitments *"totalement opaques"*
- ⚠️ This report dates the framework agreement to Nov 2023, **factually wrong** (it was 25 April 2024)

## 13.4 Algeria Invest, energy & water viability (21 Nov 2025)

- Argues **Sonelgaz has published no feasibility study** comparing fossil vs solar-integrated models → *"L'Algérie investit à l'aveugle."*
- Warns of repeating the **Saudi "milky farms" failure**
- Concludes: *"Produire « vaille que coûte » n'est pas une fuite en avant"* and *"l'absence de méthode"*

## 13.5 Peer-reviewed criticism of the Qatar model

- **MDPI *Sustainability* (Dec 2024), "Developing the Desert: How Qatar Achieved Dairy Self-Sufficiency Through Baladna"**, documents government support (vaccinations, healthcare, partial animal feed support). Concludes: *"Baladna's dependence on government support raises concerns about long-term sustainability... **The replicability of this model in other countries may be limited, particularly outside petro-developmental economies, where state support is less prevalent.**"*, **the single most on-point academic caution for the international strategy**
- **MDPI *Water* (2020), "A Rentier State under Blockade"**, allocating ~a third of Qatar's water to agriculture (0.1% of GDP, <1% employment) is *"a massive allocation of limited (and fast depleting) freshwater resources to a sector generating so little economic benefit"*; conflates *"food security"* with *"food self-sufficiency"*
- **MDPI *Water* (Apr 2024)**, Qatar imports 7,530 m³/yr virtual water vs exports 1,450; net importer 6,090 m³/yr; water dependency index 56%
- **Peer-reviewed volume on "Ethics and food security in Qatar" (2025)**, brine disposal ~175 tonnes of salt per day; Qatar's earlier land acquisitions (250,000 ha Sudan, 40,000 ha Kenya) were **never developed**
- **Historic press (Orient XXI / Le Monde, 2018-2020)**, freshwater reserves shrinking 100M m³/year; 92% of Qatari groundwater goes to farming; geologist Alain Gachet: *"Committing hydraulic suicide for political reasons and not for reasons of survival, I find that a little hard to swallow"*

## 13.6 Structural risk evident in Baladna's own filings

- **Feed import dependence**, up to USD 100m/yr from the USA (2019); only mitigated by Al Dahra MoU (2026) and Algeria's own arable land
- **Closed/protected market position**, 96.1% of Qatari fresh milk, 92.5% of UHT
- **Government tender reliance**, a QAR 100M evaporated-milk government tender contributes meaningfully to revenue; H1 2026 growth attributed to *"higher dairy sales under the Company's government supply arrangements"*
- **Abandoned projects & write-offs**, Malaysia terminated, Nigeria retracted, Philippines silent, Egypt dormant; QAR 35.8M written off
- **Executive turnover**, CEO changed Aug 2025; a Group CEO / Qatar CEO split created within months

## 13.7 On Syria specifically

**No documented criticism of Baladna's Syria project from rights organisations, sanctions lawyers or mainstream investigative outlets was found.** The 1.3%-of-national-territory land lease, the Euphrates Basin location, the ownership link to the Khayyat family, and land purchase consideration from private owners are all reported fact, but no source frames these as contested. **This is a genuine evidence gap, not an all-clear.**

## 13.8 Key structural risks to Algeria's position

| Risk | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Fiscal** | Net exposure not computable, debt/equity split contradictory and guarantee issuer undisclosed. Capital commitments tripled in H1 2026, so exposure is growing faster than documentation |
| **Water** | Fossil Albien aquifer in a desert; 2.5-4bn m³/yr at full capacity; no published EIA; no basin-level water plan |
| **Energy** | Grid connection unresolved; no feasibility study on fossil vs solar; Sonelgaz mobile turbines as a stopgap |
| **Technology dependence** | GEA (processing), Valmont/AFKO (irrigation), foreign consultancy, little genuine local integration despite official emphasis on "taux d'intégration locale élevé" |
| **Execution** | 270,000 head in a desert with imported-water dependence; airlift of 30,000 head is unprecedented in scale |
| **Related-party** | Urbacon (metal framework) is a Baladna Group company, not an independent contractor, QR 324.9M advanced by H1 2026 |
| **Market** | 20-year ONIL offtake at a time of zero revenue and no market-tested unit cost; analysts question whether local cost can beat imports |
| **Counterparty priority** | GRAIN: Baladna is a Qatari strategic company; its stated first priority is Qatar's food security |
